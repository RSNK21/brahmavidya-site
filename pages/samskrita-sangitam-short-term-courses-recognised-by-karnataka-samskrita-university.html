Saṃskṛta Saṅgītam

Indian classical music as a complete knowledge system, where theory (lakṣaṇa) and practice (lakṣya) grow together.

Indian classical music is not merely sound and performance. It is a profound knowledge tradition rooted in Sanskrit śāstra, where theory (lakṣaṇa) and practice (lakṣya) exist in a living, inseparable relationship. Musical depth emerges when technical mastery is supported by philosophical and textual understanding.

The Saṃskṛta Saṅgītam programme revives this whole way of learning. Through a structured, graded curriculum, learners engage with classical texts, Sanskrit foundations, voice culture, rāga and tāla systems, and authentic performance methods. The course cultivates musicians who sing and play with insight, precision and inner awareness.

Led by senior scholars and accomplished musicians nurtured in the guru–śiṣya paramparā, the programme is a rare chance to experience Indian classical music as a complete knowledge system rather than performance training alone.

The course runs in four levels, each an independent short-term course. We recommend taking them in order, but you may enter at any level that suits your proficiency.

An overview of the courses

Lakṣaṇa (theory)

  • Introduction to basic Saṃskṛta
  • An orientation to the study of Saṅgīta Śāstra
  • Bṛhaddeśī
  • Saṅgītaratnākara of Śārṅgadeva
  • Rāgamālā of Puṇḍarīka Viṭṭhala
  • Caturdaṇḍīprakāśikā of Veṅkaṭamakhin

Texts from three eras, Prācīna (ancient), Madhya (medieval) and Ādhunika (modern), are introduced step by step across the four levels to build a foundation in these śāstra granthas.

Lakṣya (performance)

Taught in graded steps across the four levels:

  • Prathamābhyāsa, the tāla systems, tāla abhyāsa and tālālaṅkāra abhyāsa
  • Svarajatis (jatisvaras), varṇas and kṛtis of rising difficulty, by composers in several languages including Saṃskṛta and Kannada
  • A few rare and uncommon compositions
  • Composing music for musical, dance and drama features
  • Accompanying vocal and instrumental sessions
  • Performing lakṣya and discussing its lakṣaṇa as a natural part of learning

Our aim: a devoted student who completes the levels can give technically and artistically rich performances of 30 to 60 minutes, depending on the level reached.
