About Brahmavidya

A Mysuru foundation dedicated to the research, promotion and development of Indian arts since {{founded}}.

Brahmavidya was established in {{founded}} and has worked for the Indian arts for more than {{years_active}} years. It rests on the belief that all Indian arts are expressions of Brahmavidyā, a sacred pursuit of Truth (Satya), Auspiciousness (Śubha) and Beauty (Sundara). Our vision embraces the plurality, purpose and spiritual destination of art as a form of worship and self-realisation.

Our main focus is rigorous academic research across the core and allied subjects of Indian knowledge systems. Brahmavidya also promotes and teaches Bharatanatyam, Karnataka (Carnatic) music, Yoga and Sanskrit.

Our core activities

Key initiatives

Research centre of Karnataka Samskrit University

Brahmavidya is recognised as a research centre in Saṅgīta, Nāṭya, Yoga and Ayurveda by Karnataka Samskrit University, a NAAC-accredited university. Students and researchers gain access to accredited degrees and certification.

Short-term courses

Short-term courses recognised by Karnataka Samskrit University in Sanskrit-based Saṅgīta, Nāṭya, Yoga and Ayurveda. These practical and academic courses add valuable qualifications for students and researchers.

See the courses →

MoU with Fondazione Giorgio Cini

An MoU with the Fondazione Giorgio Cini, Venice, for the dissemination of knowledge in the Indological manuscripts preserved there. Our scholars are working on the Mataṅga manuscripts, historically and musicologically important texts of Indian musicology. The publication will bring together eight Mataṅga Bharata texts with translations, critical editions and scholarly introductions, prepared according to Bhāratīya Saṃśodhana Paddhati (BhaSamPa), under the copyright of Brahmavidya.

Indian Musicology Day

Brahmavidya conceived and hosts Indian Musicology Day on 9 May each year: research seminars, innovative concerts, books on Indological subjects, the Śāstra Sūrī and Mahāmahopādhyāya Dr. R. Sathyanarayana Commemorative Awards, and documentaries. The day celebrates all knowledge systems of Bhārata: Veda, Saṃskṛta, Ayurveda, Jyotiṣa, Tantra and cognate studies.

On 9 May 2026 we launched the Abdipūrva celebrations leading to the centenary. Seminars, workshops, research discussions, Vākyārtha Sabhās and concerts continue through the year, culminating in the centenary on 9 May 2027. Watch the talks and concerts on our YouTube channel, Naada Nandana.

Bharata Śālā

Our cultural hub in Mysuru, built on the principles of the Nāṭyaśāstra, houses a well-equipped nāṭyaśālā and serves as a guru-gṛha, a residential learning space for our students.

Commemorative Art Festival

Held every year on 18 July to honour His Highness Jayachamaraja Wadiyar, the last Maharaja of Mysore. This national festival features eminent dignitaries and musicians and the conferment of the Chitprabhānanda Award. It also marks Karnataka Vāggeyakāra Day (Composers' Day), celebrating Karnataka's great composers.

Vākyārtha Sabhā

A distinctive academic seminar for the study and interpretation of classical texts on music and dance, held every year in an intimate guru-gṛha setting.

Academic leadership

Guided by a lineage of scholarship

Brahmavidya was shaped by the vision and guidance of Mahāmahopādhyāya Padmaśrī Dr. R. Sathyanarayana, a globally respected Indologist and one of its founding trustees. Until his passing he remained a guiding force for Brahmavidya's scholarly ethos and its commitment to classical Indian knowledge systems. His legacy continues to inspire our academic and artistic work.

The institution is now guided by an academic body of experienced scholars and practitioners in music, dance, Sanskrit and Indian philosophy, who carry his vision forward. Read about Dr. R. Sathyanarayana or meet our faculty.
